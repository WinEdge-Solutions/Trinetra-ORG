Frequently Asked Questions on Protection & Confidentiality

Clear, jargon-free explanations of your rights, legal safety as a reporter, and our operational responsibilities.

Can I be sued or harassed for reporting suspected child abuse? expand_more

No. Under Indian law (The POCSO Act 2012, Section 19(7)), no person who reports in good faith can be subjected to civil or criminal liability. Even if subsequent police inquiries establish that no abuse occurred, you are legally protected provided the report was filed in good faith.

How does Trinetra ensure my anonymous report cannot be traced back? expand_more

When 'Anonymous Submission Mode' is selected, no personal names or contact numbers are requested or recorded. Furthermore, our server architecture implements strict privacy protections: requests are processed with zero persistent PII logging, and client IP addresses are immediately reduced to one-way privacy hashes for rate limiting and never permanently stored.

What if the child is being forced to work by their own parents? expand_more

Under the Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Amendment Act, children under 14 cannot work in hazardous or commercial capacities. Trinetra's model focuses on restorative family rehabilitation: pairing destitute parents with State Social Welfare schemes (PDS, MNREGA, sponsorship grants) while securing compulsory school admission for the child through the RTE Act.

Can I follow up on the status of my report without revealing my identity? expand_more

Yes. Upon submission, your confidential report is logged directly with our field caseworker triage desk for statutory evaluation.

What happens if the local authorities refuse to file an FIR or intervene? expand_more

Trinetra maintains a panel of pro-bono High Court and Supreme Court advocates who immediately petition the National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR) and District Magistrates if field police show reluctance or negligence in child welfare emergencies.
